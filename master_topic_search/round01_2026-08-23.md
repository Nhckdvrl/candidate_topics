2. **Old problem, new LLM-era paradigm** — look for long-standing problems whose scientific study was previously bottlenecked by expert labor, human panels, manual resource construction, or narrow pipelines, and ask whether LLMs make qualitatively new experiments possible.

## Search-policy correction

A major failure mode in the previous topic search was:

```text
Paper A -> observable X
Paper B -> observable Y
X + Y -> invent a plausible deeper relation Z
```

The experiment for `Z` could be exceptionally clean while the relation itself had no strong reason to exist.

The corrected policy is:

```text
observed anomaly / unresolved old problem
        -> verify it is real and meaningful
        -> identify existing competing explanations or historical bottleneck
        -> only then ask for one decisive experiment
```

The lab's research history should be treated as evidence about **topic style**, not as a demand to stay inside Japanese linguistics. The recurring useful pattern is a concrete and distinctive object/task with a crisp research question.

# Phenomenon inventory

## P01 — Stronger problem solvers can be worse synthetic students

### Observed phenomenon

Recent educational / psychometric work uses LLMs as synthetic students to estimate item difficulty without conventional large-scale field testing. A 2026 Findings ACL study on NAEP-style items reports a striking result: some weaker models correlate **better** with real student item-difficulty patterns than stronger mathematical models. Stronger competence does not monotonically improve fidelity to student difficulty.

### Why it is surprising

The naive assumption behind synthetic-student simulation is that better reasoning should make the model a better simulator once appropriately prompted. Instead, competence can erase the error structure that makes a learner population useful for psychometrics.

### Why it matters

Classical item calibration requires real student samples and is expensive. If LLMs could replace or augment pilot populations, this would change educational measurement. But if higher model competence systematically destroys human-like error structure, the new paradigm has a fundamental limitation.

### Existing explanations

Likely explanations include model competence, prompting / persona conditioning, population heterogeneity, and training-data overlap. Recent work is already beginning to frame this as a competence-vs-fidelity problem.

### Exact open question

Do synthetic learners lose human-like item-difficulty structure as their task competence increases, even when overall score is matched?

### Cheapest decisive contrast

Need a family of models/checkpoints that spans competence while allowing score matching / controlled degradation. Compare correlation with human item-response patterns at matched aggregate accuracy.

### Meaningful-regime check

Strong: real educational datasets with human response matrices exist.

### Collision audit

**Dangerously crowded already.** 2025–2026 work is actively studying synthetic students, LLM psychometrics and competence/fidelity mismatch. Do not register without finding a sharper unresolved phenomenon.

### Verdict

**HOLD / USE AS A TEMPLATE FOR A GOOD PROBLEM SHAPE, NOT YET A TOPIC.**

## P02 — Many LLM personas may not constitute a statistical population

### Observed phenomenon

LLMs are increasingly used to instantiate many personas as synthetic respondents, annotators, experts or panel members. Recent work shows that output diversity and label variation from these synthetic crowds often fail to reproduce human population variation; repeated samples can be highly correlated.

### Why it is surprising

The practical appeal of synthetic panels is often phrased in sample-size language: generate 100 or 1,000 respondents cheaply. But conventional sampling gains rely on partially independent information. A large number of personas from one model may have a much smaller effective sample size.

### Why it matters

This is directly relevant to survey science, Delphi/expert panels, annotation, psychometrics and social simulation.

### Existing explanations

Shared model prior, mode collapse, insufficient persona conditioning, correlated decoding, and failure to represent minority subpopulations.

### Exact open question

When an LLM is used as a synthetic population, how much genuinely independent information is added by additional personas or samples?

### Cheapest decisive contrast

Estimate effective sample size from covariance / disagreement structure against matched human panels.

### Meaningful-regime check

Strong datasets exist.

### Collision audit

**Already substantially occupied** by recent work on human-label variation, synthetic crowds and effective diversity.

### Verdict

**KILL AS A NEW GENERAL TOPIC.** Retain as a warning for any future LLM-as-population proposal.

## P03 — LLM confidence can fail exactly on the disagreement cases where it is needed

### Observed phenomenon

Recent human–AI quiz / decision studies report that model confidence can be informative globally yet become weak or near-random specifically on human–AI disagreement cases, i.e. the subset where a confidence signal is supposed to arbitrate who to trust.

### Why it is surprising

A confidence score that works on ordinary cases but ceases to discriminate on the decision boundary has limited practical epistemic value.

### Why it matters

This matters for selective prediction, delegation, expert-AI collaboration and decision support.

### Existing explanations

Selection effects on disagreement cases, correlated human/model difficulty, verbalized confidence calibration, answer plausibility, and explanation quality.

### Exact open question

Is the failure on disagreement cases an unavoidable selection phenomenon, or does model confidence omit precisely the uncertainty dimension that matters for comparative arbitration?

### Cheapest decisive contrast

Need to identify whether confidence's discrimination collapse persists when item difficulty / model correctness probability is controlled, ideally with existing logged competitions rather than a large new human study.

### Meaningful-regime check

Strong: real human–AI interaction datasets exist.

### Collision audit

The headline observation is already published. A follow-up must find a specific unresolved causal question, not re-report disagreement calibration.

### Verdict

**DEEP-AUDIT.** Healthy anomaly seed; project not yet defined.

## P04 — Grokking prediction before the test accuracy jump

### Observed phenomenon

Grokking and delayed generalization remain a recurring training-dynamics phenomenon: training performance saturates long before generalization abruptly improves. Practitioners still find it difficult to know from an early run whether a model is on a trajectory toward grokking or simply memorizing indefinitely.

### Why it is surprising

The observable test jump occurs late, but the optimization trajectory may already contain information about whether a representation capable of generalization is forming.

### Why it matters

If future generalization is predictable substantially before it becomes behaviorally visible, this would affect how we understand phase transitions and how expensive experiments are stopped or continued.

### Existing explanations

Weight norm, circuit competition, simplicity bias, feature learning, representation geometry, loss-landscape transitions and mechanistic progress measures have all been proposed.

### Exact open question

Is there a robust early behavioral / optimization signature that predicts whether a run will later grok across tasks and seeds?

### Cheapest decisive contrast

This is **not yet clean** because many candidate predictors already exist. Need literature audit for a repeated empirical anomaly that current predictors fail on before considering a project.

### Meaningful-regime check

Moderate: many grokking setups are toy tasks, so significance is a serious risk.

### Collision audit

Very crowded. The broad question is not novel.

### Verdict

**HOLD.** Search only for a sharper unexplained anomaly in modern non-toy training.

# Old-problem -> LLM-paradigm-change inventory

## O01 — Psychometric item calibration without conventional field testing

### Pre-LLM problem

Estimating item difficulty, discrimination and population response patterns traditionally requires substantial student data and psychometric field testing.

### What LLMs change

LLMs can cheaply generate many responses under controlled prompting, making synthetic pretesting plausible.

### New scientific possibility

Instead of merely asking whether an LLM can solve the item, we can test whether a synthetic population preserves the **response geometry** required for psychometric inference.

### Current status

The area is already moving quickly and competence-vs-fidelity anomalies have been reported.

### Verdict

**GOOD PARADIGM EXAMPLE; PROBABLY TOO CROWDED AS A FRESH MASTER TOPIC.**

## O02 — Expert panels / Delphi-style elicitation at synthetic scale

### Pre-LLM problem

Expert elicitation is expensive and slow. Panel size is limited by expert availability, and repeated rounds are costly.

### What LLMs change

Models can emulate viewpoints, critique proposals and generate large synthetic panels.

### New scientific possibility

Potentially allows large controlled studies of consensus formation, framing and panel composition.

### Central scientific risk

Synthetic members may share one highly correlated prior, so scaling panel size may not add independent information.

### Current status

Recent synthetic-population / LLM-crowd work already directly attacks this issue.

### Verdict

**KILL AS BROAD TOPIC.** Search for a domain-specific old panel problem only if there is a new unresolved anomaly.

## O03 — Scientific-document annotation / taxonomy construction as an experimental instrument

### Pre-LLM problem

Many meta-science questions require experts to read and classify thousands of papers, citations, claims, methods or failure reasons. This strongly limits scale and often forces small manually curated datasets.

### What LLMs change

Modern LLMs can produce structured annotations, rationales and candidate taxonomies over very large corpora, with human auditing concentrated on a smaller subset.

### Potential paradigm change

This may permit scientific questions about **field-wide dynamics** that were previously too labor-intensive, rather than simply replacing a citation-intent classifier.

Examples of potentially meaningful questions:

- How often do later papers actually validate, contradict or silently abandon the central empirical claims of influential papers?
- Are published limitations predictive of where later work fails, or are later failures mostly outside the original authors' anticipated failure modes?
- How does a claimed mechanism evolve through citation chains: replicated, reinterpreted, weakened, or converted into folklore?

### Why this route is promising

The LLM acts as a scalable research instrument over a historical scientific corpus. The object of study is science, not the LLM itself.

### Biggest danger

It can easily collapse into a noisy annotation / dataset paper. We need a **sharp, falsifiable meta-scientific phenomenon**, preferably one with a small gold audit and an old literature question behind it.

### Exact open question

Not fixed yet.

### Verdict

**KEEP — HIGH PRIORITY FOR NEXT SEARCH ROUND.**

## O04 — Controlled stimulus generation for behavioral science

### Pre-LLM problem

Psycholinguistics, HCI and cognitive experiments often require hand-writing and validating large sets of tightly controlled stimuli. This limits factorial coverage and makes many high-dimensional interventions impractical.

### What LLMs change

LLMs can generate candidate stimuli at scale under explicit structural constraints, potentially allowing thousands of matched naturalistic cases.

### Potential paradigm change

Researchers could move from a few dozen hand-crafted stimuli to large, heterogeneous but controlled experimental panels.

### Central scientific question

Can LLM-generated stimuli preserve the intended causal manipulation while varying nuisance dimensions enough to test generality?

### Biggest danger

The topic may become methodology / data generation rather than a substantive scientific question.

### Verdict

**HOLD AS AN INSTRUMENTAL ROUTE.** Use only when attached to a strong phenomenon.

## O05 — Question / distractor construction from misconception models

### Pre-LLM problem

Writing high-quality questions and distractors requires subject-matter expertise and knowledge of common learner misconceptions. Classical automatic distractor generation relied on lexical resources, templates or narrow supervised systems.

### What LLMs change

LLMs can generate plausible incorrect reasoning traces and distractors at scale.

### Observed modern tension

Recent work suggests LLMs may be capable of generating plausible wrong reasoning while still being poor at identifying which error will be pedagogically diagnostic for real learners.

### Potential new question

Can the model's generative space of errors be used to discover **previously unmodeled misconception structure**, rather than merely generate answer options?

### Why it matters

That would turn question generation from content production into a tool for studying learner error structure.

### Biggest danger

Requires real learner-response validation and can expand into many psychometric metrics.

### Verdict

**DEEP-AUDIT.** Search historical distractor / misconception literature and current real-student validation studies.

---

## O06 — Recovering latent structure in old hand-built knowledge resources

### Pre-LLM problem

Frame semantics, lexical resources, ontologies and domain taxonomies required years of expert manual construction. Coverage and maintenance were fundamental bottlenecks.

### What LLMs change

LLMs can propose frames, relations, examples and mappings from raw corpora with much broader coverage.

### Potential paradigm change

The interesting scientific question is not whether an LLM can auto-fill FrameNet. It is whether large-scale model-induced structure reveals **stable semantic organization that manual resources could not economically observe**.

### Biggest danger

Very easy to become "automatic resource expansion," which is incremental and already crowded.

### Verdict

**HOLD.** Search only for a concrete historical unresolved structural question, not resource completion.
