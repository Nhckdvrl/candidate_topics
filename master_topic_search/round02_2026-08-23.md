# Master Topic Search — Round 02 (2026-08-23)

## Search objective

Round 02 changes the search procedure using the most useful lesson recovered from the labmate-topic investigation:

> **Do not begin from a large invented story. Begin from one paper that has already established a measurement / phenomenon / structure, then move one scientifically meaningful variable.**

Examples recovered from the lab:

```text
Hamdi:     known / unknown        → real / fictional
Kurauchi:  token-level emergence → compositional emergence
Tsujimoto: human developmental time → LM training time
Kisako:    dimension             → dimension × precision
Yano:      predict frame          → does frame-like distinction appear behaviorally?
```

The goal in this round was therefore not to brainstorm domains. It was to search 2025–2026 papers and older scientific literatures for **standing experimental footholds** and then aggressively attack the nearest one-step question.

The embodied-topic-search rules are imported explicitly:

1. no narrowing the mother question merely to dodge collision;
2. adjacent work is allowed, but the narrative angle and first experiment must still be meaningfully new;
3. first prove the phenomenon / distinction, then discuss mechanism;
4. candidates that fail collision or identification remain in the log rather than being silently forgotten;
5. only survivors get individual files under `master_topic_search/candidates/`.

# Survivor A — When Does a Fact Become Recallable?

Full candidate: [candidates/when_does_a_fact_become_recallable.md](./candidates/when_does_a_fact_become_recallable.md)

## Seed phenomenon

Calderon et al. (2026), **[Empty Shelves or Lost Keys? Recall Is the Bottleneck for Parametric Factuality](https://research.google/blog/empty-shelves-or-lost-keys-recall-is-the-bottleneck-for-parametric-factuality/)**, introduces fact-level **knowledge profiling** and demonstrates a large final-model dissociation between:

```text
fact is encoded
vs
fact is directly recallable
```

Frontier models encode most tested facts but still fail to directly recall many of them.

## One-step rotation

The seed studies final models / model scale.

Round-02 question:

> **During pretraining, does a fact become encoded before it becomes reliably recallable?**

```text
final-model knowledge state
        ↓
knowledge state over training checkpoints
```

This is close to the healthy Tsujimoto-style transformation: use an established measurement and change only the developmental axis.

## Collision attack

Factual acquisition over checkpoints already exists. In particular, **[Tracing Multilingual Factual Knowledge Acquisition in Pretraining](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.113/)** follows OLMo checkpoints and relates factual recall to training-corpus frequency.

However, the current audit did not find a work that applies the 2026 **encoding-vs-recall profile jointly at each checkpoint** and asks whether an `encoded but inaccessible` state systematically precedes usable factual recall.

## Identification attack

The largest risk is that a WikiProfile-style encoding probe measures source-like lexical continuation rather than meaningful storage.

Therefore the G-0 requires:

- at least two encoding probes per fact;
- natural facts verified in the model's actual pretraining corpus;
- semantically different direct/reverse QA for access;
- a per-fact transition observable rather than only aggregate accuracy.

## Cheapest decisive G-0

Estimate:

```text
t_encode(f)
t_recall(f)
lag(f) = t_recall(f) - t_encode(f)
```

for a few hundred natural facts over an open checkpoint family.

Kill if the lag is essentially zero, unstable to mild probe paraphrase, or visible only in synthetic injection data.

## Verdict

`KEEP — HIGH PRIORITY`

# Survivor B — Do Scientific Claims Become More Certain as They Are Cited?

Full candidate: [candidates/do_scientific_claims_become_more_certain_as_they_are_cited.md](./candidates/do_scientific_claims_become_more_certain_as_they_are_cited.md)

## Historical phenomenon

Greenberg's 2009 BMJ paper, **[How citation distortions create unfounded authority](https://www.bmj.com/content/339/bmj.b2680)**, manually reconstructed a 242-paper / 675-citation biomedical belief network and documented **citation transmutation**: a scientific statement can travel from tentative hypothesis toward asserted fact without new supporting data.

The attractive mother question is therefore old and concrete:

> **Does a hypothesis become a fact merely by being cited?**

## Why LLMs may genuinely change the experiment

Historically, generalizing Greenberg required humans to identify the **same semantic claim** across many papers, compare the epistemic strength of each restatement, and determine whether new evidence had actually been added.

Modern full-text corpora + citation graphs + LLM semantic matching make many-claim longitudinal analysis practical enough to attempt.

This is the desired old-problem → new-instrument pattern: the LLM is an experimental instrument, not the benchmarked object.

## Collision attack

This area is not empty:

- Small et al. (2019), **[Citations and certainty](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/scient/v118y2019i3d10.1007_s11192-019-03016-z.html)**, already shows that later / more-cited biomedical citances are less hedged.
- Prieto et al. (2020), **[Data-driven classification of the certainty of scholarly assertions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7182025/)**, provides automated certainty categories.
- Sarol et al. (2025), **[Automatic Identification of Citation Distortions in Biomedical Literature](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pra2.1281)**, automates parts of the original Greenberg case study.

So the broad claims “citation language changes certainty” and “NLP can detect distortions” are already occupied.

The surviving question is stronger and more specific in scientific object, not artificially narrower:

> **holding the proposition fixed, does certainty increase specifically on citation edges where no new evidence for that proposition is added?**

That is the causal content of Greenberg's famous claim and is not answered by aggregate hedging frequency.

## Cheapest decisive G-0

1. reproduce known Greenberg transmutation edges;
2. validate high-precision claim-equivalence matching;
3. collect 20–50 new biomedical claim cascades;
4. label each restatement edge as `new evidence` vs `no new evidence`;
5. compare certainty change under the two conditions;
6. human-audit roughly 150–300 critical edges.

Kill if claim equivalence or evidence status cannot be labeled reliably, or if certainty drift disappears when proposition identity and added evidence are controlled.

## Verdict

`KEEP — HIGH PRIORITY; identification/data risk higher than Survivor A`

# Survivor C — Is Negative Behavioral Adaptation Intrinsically Harder?

Full candidate: [candidates/is_negative_behavioral_adaptation_intrinsically_harder.md](./candidates/is_negative_behavioral_adaptation_intrinsically_harder.md)

## Seed anomaly

Qin et al., ACL 2026 Best Resource Paper, **[ImplicitMemBench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1301/)**, reports a dramatic cross-model difference:

```text
inhibition: 17.6%
preference: 75.0%
```

The paper interprets this as a persistent inability to transform negative experience into later behavioral suppression.

## Critical attack on the seed interpretation

The positive and negative conditions are **not matched versions of the same task**. Figure 4 compares pairs such as:

```text
jargon avoidance   vs directory preference
API distrust       vs filetype preference
context behavior   vs brevity preference
```

Thus `feedback sign` and `task family / prior strength / output structure` change simultaneously.

The anomaly is real, but “inhibition itself is the bottleneck” is not yet cleanly identified.

## Exact one-step question

> **If action space, baseline preference, experience count/order, outcome magnitude and test are all matched, does negative experience still produce much weaker adaptation than positive experience?**

The first experiment uses arbitrary balanced `A/B` actions and changes only:

```text
learn to select A
vs
learn to avoid A
```

with identity swaps and symmetric outcomes.

## Collision attack

Learning-from-failure, Reflexion, negative-trajectory tuning and agent-memory work are crowded. But those papers primarily propose mechanisms to improve downstream agents.

This candidate is not “make agents learn from failure.” It asks a more basic behavioral question before method design.

The current audit did not find a prior modern-LLM study with the required **identity-swapped matched positive/negative conditioning design**.

## Cheapest decisive G-0

A small factorial dataset of 40–80 base situations is sufficient for the first test.

Kill the fundamental-inhibition story if the 57-point benchmark gap collapses once tasks and priors are matched.

A strong positive means a genuine acquisition-vs-suppression asymmetry; a strong negative means a recent headline benchmark phenomenon was largely task-family confounding. Both are informative.

## Verdict

`KEEP — HIGH PRIORITY`

# Strong lead attacked and downgraded — contextual entrainment over training time

## Initial attraction

ACL 2026 work on **contextual entrainment** reports an unusual scaling split: larger models become better at rejecting semantically incorrect context while becoming more prone to mechanically copying arbitrary irrelevant tokens.

A labmate-style move seemed obvious:

```text
model-size axis
    ↓
training-time axis
```

Question:

> Do semantic filtering and mechanical copying emerge together during pretraining and later bifurcate?

## Why it was downgraded

The deeper collision search found that important halves of the proposed story are already occupied:

- training dynamics of copying / induction have already been studied, including grokking-like emergence;
- checkpoint work already studies how in-context vs parametric knowledge use changes through training.

At that point the candidate risked becoming exactly the bad pattern:

```text
Paper A measures X over training
+ Paper B reports X/Y at scale
→ invent a relationship between their trajectories
```

Without an independent reason that the joint trajectory should reveal a meaningful scientific object, this is not yet strong enough.

## Verdict

`HOLD — DO NOT CREATE CANDIDATE FILE`

# Killed lead — reviewer criticism overlap / coverage

## Initial attraction

Old peer-review studies report low agreement among reviewers; OpenReview now provides enormous multi-review corpora, and LLM semantic matching could make critique-level comparison much easier.

Potential question:

> Do independent reviewers discover the same substantive weaknesses, or is peer-review value primarily additive coverage?

## Collision

This is already directly occupied. Recent work explicitly measures **review coverage and redundancy**, including a 2024 causal study of reviewer-group diversity over roughly 5,000 submissions, and 2026 AI-reviewer studies already quantify criticism-level reviewer overlap.

## Verdict

`KILL — exact scientific object substantially occupied`

# Killed lead — large-scale multiple scientific discovery / rediscovery

## Initial attraction

Merton's classic “multiple discoveries” problem seems ideal for LLM semantic matching: find independent papers that make the same discovery without citation contact.

## Collision

The area has already moved far beyond anecdotal cases:

- 2025 **Is Science Inevitable?** studies multiple discoveries over 40M papers;
- 2026 Science Advances work introduces embedding-based disruptiveness measures and explicitly uncovers simultaneous breakthroughs.

An LLM-semantic version would currently be method substitution rather than a new scientific question.

## Verdict

`KILL — broad question already answered at scale`

# Killed / do-not-return lead — generic Agentic RL feedback internalization

The broad question

> does Agentic RL merely learn to use feedback or genuinely internalize new knowledge from interaction?

remains scientifically interesting, but 2026 work is rapidly occupying feedback-token supervision, experience internalization, agentic distillation and skill acquisition. It also lies in exactly the fast-moving generic Agentic-RL region the advisor warned against.

Do not keep repackaging it with narrower token types or task families.

`KILL AS MASTER-TOPIC CANDIDATE`

# Killed lead — temporal forgetting: erased capability or inaccessible capability?

A tempting recent training-dynamics observation is that many final errors were answered correctly by earlier checkpoints.

But asking whether those capabilities are “truly erased” or merely inaccessible immediately creates the same conceptual-identification problem seen in archived Topics: latent recovery depends on prompts, inference, alternative routes and task inference. Catastrophic-forgetting / latent-capability recovery work is already dense.

`KILL — identification complexity grows faster than scientific clarity`

# Search lessons from this round

## 1. The labmate recipe is productive only when the seed already owns the phenomenon

The good form is:

```text
paper establishes surprising state / measurement
        ↓
change one meaningful axis
        ↓
ask a question whose NO answer is also clean
```

The bad form is:

```text
paper A studies X
paper B studies Y
        ↓
maybe X and Y have an elegant relationship
```

The contextual-entrainment lead nearly fell into the second form and was therefore downgraded.

## 2. A recent benchmark can be a seed even when we do not want a benchmark paper

ImplicitMemBench is valuable not because we should add more benchmark tasks, but because it reports a large anomaly whose causal interpretation can be tested with a **much smaller** matched experiment.

## 3. Old problems become attractive when the old paper itself tells us why scale was impossible

The citation-transmutation direction is stronger than generic “LLM for scientometrics” because there is:

- an old named phenomenon;
- a manual canonical study;
- a precise labor bottleneck;
- a new scalable instrument;
- a direct falsification path.

## 4. Do not reward a lead merely because it has no exact paper title match

Reviewer-overlap and multiple-discovery ideas initially looked fresh under superficial keyword search. Broader science-of-science searches showed that the scientific objects themselves are already occupied.
