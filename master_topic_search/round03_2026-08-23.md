# Survivor D — Does Methodological Information Decay Along Shortcut-Citation Chains?

Full candidate: [candidates/does_methodological_information_decay_along_shortcut_citation_chains.md](./candidates/does_methodological_information_decay_along_shortcut_citation_chains.md)

## Seed phenomenon

The 2024 PLOS Biology study **Shortcut citations in the methods section: Frequency, problems, and strategies for responsible reuse** examined more than 750 papers in neuroscience, biology, and psychiatry and found that more than 90% used shortcut citations to stand in for methodological descriptions.

The authors then manually followed shortcut-citation chains for 15 parent papers and encountered recurring failures:

- inaccessible / difficult-to-identify cited resources;
- cited sources that did not actually contain enough methodological detail;
- sources that themselves pointed to another source;
- old references that might not reflect the method currently performed;
- chains whose extra hops amplified the difficulty of recovering a usable procedure.

The chain study was deliberately exploratory; it established the existence and types of problems rather than their prevalence or parameter-level transmission dynamics.

## One-step question

> **When the same experimental method is transmitted paper → paper → paper through shortcut citations, are critical implementation units preserved, explicitly changed, silently changed, or lost?**

The distinction from generic methods underreporting is crucial. The proposed object is **transmission fidelity across citation generations**.

## Why LLMs may change the research regime

Historically, this requires experts to locate method passages, recursively follow shortcut citations, extract heterogeneous parameters / procedural steps and align them across prose.

LLMs can now act as a structured-extraction and semantic-alignment instrument, with a modest human gold audit, making dozens of multi-hop method lineages feasible rather than 15 manually reconstructed parent papers.

The LLM is not the object of the paper.

## Collision attack

The methods-reproducibility literature already establishes:

- incomplete methods reporting;
- indirect / shortcut citation problems;
- difficulty reconstructing procedures;
- automated scientific information extraction.

Therefore none of those can be the headline novelty.

The current search did **not** find a study that measures same-method, parameter/step-level preservation and silent modification as a function of shortcut-citation generation over many method lineages.

If such a study is found later, kill rather than narrow to one journal or one reagent.

## Cheapest decisive G-0

Choose 2–4 well-structured biomedical / neuroscience method families and reconstruct roughly 20–50 genuine shortcut-citation chains.

Track critical units such as:

```text
concentration / dose
duration / temperature
instrument / software version
threshold / preprocessing parameter
critical procedural step / order
```

For every edge:

```text
preserved
explicitly modified
silently inconsistent
omitted / unrecoverable
```

Primary result: retention and silent-inconsistency rate versus chain depth.

Kill if inheritance cannot be distinguished from credit citation, if alignment needs expert annotation at every edge, or if depth does not predict meaningful information loss once method / age are controlled.

## Verdict

`KEEP — PROVISIONAL TOP-5`

This is the strongest Round-03 old-problem / new-instrument candidate.

# Survivor E — Do Language-Model Memories Consolidate With Age?

Full candidate: [candidates/do_language_model_memories_consolidate_with_age.md](./candidates/do_language_model_memories_consolidate_with_age.md)

## Seed phenomenon

Krasheninnikov, Turner & Krueger, **Fresh in Memory: Training-Order Recency Is Linearly Encoded in Language Model Activations** (ICLR 2026), trains on six sequential entity datasets and establishes that model activations preserve information about *when* an entity was learned.

The paper reports:

- the six dataset centroids align in exact temporal order along a nearly linear direction;
- early-vs-late entities can be linearly decoded at roughly 90% accuracy;
- the signal generalizes beyond probe-training entities;
- the model can be trained to report training stage;
- simple activation magnitude, loss and confidence do not explain the signal.

This gives a clean standing representation-level object: **training-order recency**.

## One-step question

> **For facts that are equally well remembered now, does their acquisition age predict how easily later contradictory training can overwrite them?**

This is the natural representation → behavior rotation:

```text
model encodes when knowledge was learned
        ↓
does that acquisition history change future plasticity?
```

## Collision attack

Catastrophic forgetting and knowledge editing are crowded. Recent work already maps item-level forgetting during post-training and develops many ways to alter facts.

But the current search did not find the exact identification design:

```text
experimentally known acquisition age
+ matched current fact strength
+ same final model
+ identical contradictory update
→ compare overwrite dynamics
```

The candidate must stay at this level. “A new knowledge-editing method” is not the project.

## Cheapest decisive G-0

Reuse the controlled sequential-entity setup from Fresh in Memory.

At the final checkpoint:

1. retain facts that are all currently correct;
2. match subsets across D1…D6 on answer confidence / loss / robustness;
3. branch from the same final model;
4. give each selected fact an identically formatted contradictory update;
5. measure steps / gradient budget until the new value reliably dominates.

Primary observable:

```text
acquisition stage / recency projection
vs
overwrite curve or flip budget
```

The sign is intentionally not assumed: older facts may be harder **or easier** to rewrite.

Kill if age has no effect after matching current strength, if the effect follows dataset identity rather than stage, or if it is entirely explained by baseline confidence / loss.

## Important distinction from Candidate A

Candidate A asks:

```text
When during acquisition does encoding become usable recall?
```

Candidate E asks:

```text
After facts are already learned, does acquisition history determine future plasticity under conflict?
```

They are related learning-dynamics questions but not duplicates.

## Verdict

`KEEP — PROVISIONAL TOP-5`

This is the strongest new Round-03 seed-paper / one-step-rotation candidate.

# Lead attacked and killed — source-label trust bias → alignment origin

## Initial seed

ACL 2026 work reports a controlled label effect: identical material is judged differently depending on whether it is labeled `human-authored` or `AI-generated`; humans and LLM-as-a-judge systems can show human-source preference.

A natural first thought was:

> **At what stage does this human-like heuristic enter the model — base pretraining, instruction tuning, or preference alignment?**

## Why killed

Deeper collision search found the broader causal story is already directly occupied:

- **User–Assistant Bias in LLMs** (Findings ACL 2026) studies role-source bias across base, instruction and reasoning models and includes controlled post-training evidence that human-preference alignment can amplify the bias;
- other 2026 work directly compares pre/post-alignment numerical bias.

At that point changing the cue from role label to “human vs AI” source label would mostly be substituting one bias type.

`KILL — would survive only by narrowing the bias subtype`

# Lead attacked and killed — spacing effect for factual retention

## Initial attraction

The classical spacing effect is exactly the sort of old cognitive phenomenon that can be transplanted into controlled LM learning:

> same total exposures, massed vs spaced schedule → different long-term retention?

This would have been a clean training-dynamics question.

## Why killed

A very recent August 2026 paper, **When to Review: Spaced Repetition for Continual Pre-Training of Language Models**, already directly imports spaced-repetition scheduling into continual language-model pretraining. Adjacent 2025 work also uses spacing-inspired continual-learning curricula.

The general mother question has therefore entered an active method literature. A new project would likely have to retreat to one fact type / schedule / model.

`KILL — collision arrived essentially now; do not chase it`

# Lead attacked and killed — preprint → publication claim drift at scale

## Initial attraction

A strong old meta-science question is whether peer review / publication systematically changes the central scientific claim, especially its certainty or direction.

LLMs make semantic claim matching across preprint and published versions easy enough to scale.

## Why killed

A July 2026 study has already used an LLM pipeline to track primary-claim changes across **72,644 biomedical studies** from preprint to publication, including major/minor changes and changes in certainty.

That means the exact “LLM unlocks large-scale preprint→publication claim evolution” paradigm is already occupied.

`KILL — exact new-instrument story has already happened`

# Lead attacked and killed — evaluator vs generator / listener vs speaker asymmetry

The appealing broad question:

> **Why can a model recognize good / bad outputs better than it can generate them?**

is already crowded by judge-vs-generation, pragmatic listener-vs-speaker and self-evaluation research in 2025–2026. Even the direction of the supposed asymmetry is not universal across tasks.

`KILL — broad object crowded, any rescue would be task-specific`

# Search lessons

## 1. “Five candidates” must not become a quota that corrupts the selection rule

This round killed several candidates that could easily have been made superficially novel by changing the exact bias / schedule / corpus. They were not promoted.

## 2. Fresh recent papers can invalidate an otherwise excellent old-problem rotation overnight

Spacing was a good-looking idea until the August 2026 continual-pretraining paper appeared. This reinforces the need for exact collision search immediately before registration and again before implementation.

## 3. Representation → behavioral consequence is a productive lab-style rotation

`Fresh in Memory` is especially healthy because the seed's measurement is already strong and the next question is not “can we probe something else?” but:

> **does the measured property change what the model subsequently does under a natural intervention?**

## 4. Old-problem / new-LLM-instrument candidates need a real historical bottleneck

Method citation-chain decay survives because the canonical study was explicitly manual and small at the chain level. The proposed LLM use changes the number of lineages that can be inspected; it is not merely replacing an old classifier.

Do **not** register all five.

The next phase should run extremely small G-0 designs on the top candidates, starting with the experiments whose negative result is most decisive. In parallel, breadth-first search can continue; the existence of five survivors is not evidence that the search space is exhausted.
