# Archive lessons applied in this round

The most relevant historical failures are:

### Topic 01 — empty-cell speculation

A clean adjacent question did not imply the hypothesized behavior/representation temporal decoupling existed.

**Lesson:** a seed paper plus an untested axis is question generation, not evidence.

### Topic 07 / Topic 14 — motivating phenomenon != explanatory factor

The motivating effects were real and strong, but the proposed architecture / temporal axis produced weak or null effects.

**Lesson:** a large seed anomaly does not make our favorite causal interpretation likely.

### Topic 12 — stable structures != meaningful mapping

Two stable profiles correlated due to broad geometry rather than local correspondence.

**Lesson:** representation X plus behavioral variable Y is not enough to justify a project asking whether X controls Y.

### Topic 05 — complexity smell

An intuitive latent distinction became less identifiable as controls accumulated.

**Lesson:** if the headline requires many semantic labels / exclusions before it means what we claim, the candidate must be simplified or dropped.

### Topic 06 / Topic 10 — prerequisite and meaningful regime

A natural question is not enough if the chosen system cannot instantiate the prerequisite phenomenon in a meaningful regime.

# Candidate 1 — Do Scientific Claims Become More Certain as They Are Cited?

## Revised evidence status

`Tier S / strong external phenomenon`

This is stronger than initially appreciated.

The object is not only Greenberg's famous 2009 biomedical case. Independent later case studies also report analogous citation distortion / transmutation patterns, including long-lived ferritin-capacity claims and recent analyses of globally repeated irrigation statistics whose uncertainty/ranges become sharpened into precise factual numbers through citation.

Greenberg's original study already demonstrated a direct `hypothesis → fact` transition through citation without new supporting data in a 242-paper / 675-citation claim network.

Sarol et al. (ASIS&T 2025) then showed that modern NLP / LLM pipelines can partially automate Greenberg-style distortion analysis, while the harder `invention` / transmutation cases remain challenging.

This is therefore not:

```text
maybe citation causes certainty drift
```

It is:

```text
citation transmutation demonstrably occurs
→ how general is it?
→ can it be detected and prevented at scale?
```

## Excitement test

Positive headline:

> **Scientific claims systematically become more certain as they propagate through citation, even when the literature adds no new evidence for the claim.**

This is strong enough. It changes how citation counts, reviews, apparent consensus and literature synthesis should be interpreted.

## Method-opening test

Immediate lever:

```text
citing claim
vs
cited source claim
vs
actual supporting evidence
```

Natural method:

- evidence-conditioned citation verifier;
- epistemic-status preservation checker;
- claim-provenance graph that flags certainty inflation / dead-end support;
- scholarly-writing guard that prevents stronger claims than cited evidence warrants.

Generic provenance systems are already appearing, e.g. PaperTrail (CHI 2026), so the method cannot merely highlight evidence. It must target **citation semantic fidelity and epistemic-status drift**.

## Identification concern

The naive G0 requires:

```text
same claim
+ certainty level
+ evidence-added / no-evidence-added
```

This can become a Topic-05-style label stack.

The next design pass must simplify the domain / edge type so that absence of new primary evidence is as close to structural as possible and validate claim equivalence at high precision.

## Verdict

`STRONG_KEEP`

Current rank: **#1**.

# Candidate 2 — Does Methodological Information Decay Along Shortcut-Citation Chains?

## Revised evidence status

`Tier A/B`

The 2024 PLOS Biology seed is strong:

- >90% of examined papers used shortcut citations;
- manual chain tracing found inaccessible resources, incomplete method descriptions, recursive shortcuts and possibly outdated method sources;
- the authors explicitly state that extra chain steps can amplify these problems.

But our original claim that method information should decline **monotonically with citation depth** is still an unnecessary speculative law.

## Reframe

Replace:

> Does method information decay with citation depth?

with:

> **When a paper claims to perform a method “as described previously,” can the implementation actually be reconstructed from the cited lineage, and how often does the current paper silently diverge from what it cites?**

This asks about a concrete reproducibility failure rather than betting the project on a monotonic curve.

## Excitement test

Strong positive headline:

> **A substantial fraction of scientific methods claimed to be inherited from prior papers are not reconstructible from their cited lineage or contain undeclared implementation divergence.**

That is more consequential than “retention falls with hop count.”

## Method-opening test

Immediate lever: the citation chain itself.

Natural methods:

- protocol-lineage resolver;
- shortcut-citation linter;
- automatic method-diff system;
- missing-detail / silent-modification detector;
- system that reconstructs a self-contained method description from the lineage.

Intern-Atlas (2026) already shows method-level evolution graphs over >1M papers are feasible. This is useful infrastructure evidence but also means generic methodology genealogy is not novel. The method contribution must focus on **implementation fidelity and reproducibility repair**.

## Main risk

If “critical method units” must be hand-designed afresh for every method family, the project becomes annotation-heavy and violates the solo / six-month bar.

## Verdict

`KEEP_REFRAME`

Current rank: **#2**.

# Candidate 3 — Is Negative Behavioral Adaptation Intrinsically Harder?

## Revised evidence status

`Tier B`

ImplicitMemBench's `17.6% inhibition vs 75.0% preference` asymmetry is a real result across many modern models.

However, the illustrated inhibition and preference comparisons differ in task family at the same time as feedback sign:

```text
jargon avoidance vs directory preference
API distrust vs filetype preference
context behavior vs brevity preference
```

Therefore:

```text
headline anomaly is strong
but
feedback sign / inhibition is only one plausible explanation
```

This maps almost exactly to the archive warning from Topics 07 and 14.

## Excitement test

If the fully matched effect survives, the headline is genuinely strong:

> **LLMs can automatically acquire beneficial habits from experience but systematically fail to suppress equally simple actions that experience shows are harmful.**

## Method-opening test

The lever is also obvious:

- suppressive / inhibition-aware implicit memory;
- outcome-gated action bias;
- post-training specifically for first-attempt behavioral adaptation after negative experience.

However, ICLR 2025 already has a general method paper, *Learning from negative feedback, or positive feedback or both*, demonstrating stable gradient-based learning from negative feedback. Thus a future method must specifically solve **implicit behavioral adaptation after experience**, not generic learning from negative examples.

## Decision logic

Because the matched G0 is tiny, it is still worth running.

But do **not** call this a project before G0.

If the matched gap is not large and cross-model consistent, archive immediately. No representation probes, no task expansion, no search for a favorable environment.

## Verdict

`CHEAP_G0_ONLY`

Current rank: **#3**, but not yet a full survivor.

# Candidate 4 — When Does a Fact Become Recallable?

## Why the current formulation fails the new bar

The seed phenomenon is excellent: `Empty Shelves or Lost Keys?` shows that encoded knowledge can be inaccessible.

But the proposed project adds a new axis:

```text
encoded-but-inaccessible exists in final models
        ↓
maybe facts pass through this state developmentally during training
```

This remains a speculative temporal bridge. Topic 01 already taught us not to confuse an empty developmental axis with evidence that a trajectory exists.

### More importantly: the method opening has moved

July 2026 work, **Can a Language Model Learn Facts Continually in Its Weights?**, directly studies the actionable recitation→use problem.

It finds:

- bare-statement factual training leaves a `27.4` point recitation-to-use gap;
- broad / diverse restatements reduce the gap to `5.4` points;
- broadly trained facts also survive later sequential writes much better.

So the natural intervention:

> change the learning experience so factual knowledge becomes usable

is already an active result, not merely an open method space waiting behind our temporal description.

## Excitement test

A positive lag result would be interesting, but much less exciting than the seed paper's existing recall-bottleneck finding or the new continual-learning result.

It risks becoming:

> facts become usable somewhat later than they become recitable.

Then what?

The strongest “then what?” is already partly answered by data-diversity work.

## Verdict

`KILL CURRENT FORMULATION`

Remove from active shortlist.

Do not rescue with narrower fact types / checkpoint families.

# Candidate 5 — Do Language-Model Memories Consolidate With Age?

## Why the current formulation fails the evidence bar

`Fresh in Memory` establishes:

> acquisition recency is linearly encoded in activations.

Our proposed question asks:

> does acquisition age control overwrite resistance?

That is elegant, but the relation itself has no direct behavioral support in the seed.

This is exactly the kind of:

```text
representation X exists
→ maybe X controls behavior Y
```

bridge that the archive now tells us not to promote without external evidence.

The fact that the result would be exciting and leave a nice method opening does **not** solve the existence-risk problem.

Recent 2026 continual-factual-learning papers are also rapidly mapping sequential write/forget/access behavior. Betting months on a new age→plasticity relation simply because the experiment is clean is not justified.

## What would revive it

Any external result showing one of the following would materially change the prior:

- early-vs-late learned items differ in forgetting susceptibility after current strength is matched;
- recency-marker intervention changes later conflicting update behavior;
- item-level plasticity robustly depends on known acquisition order.

Without such evidence, do not run the full candidate experiment.

## Verdict

`HOLD_FOR_EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE`

Remove from active shortlist.

# Main lesson

The labmate formula:

```text
strong seed paper + one-step rotation
```

is a **topic-generation heuristic**, not a selection guarantee.

From now on a one-step question is promoted only when:

```text
strong evidence the scientific object exists
+ positive result is genuinely exciting
+ obvious lever/method opening
+ first experiment is identifying and simple
```

Otherwise it stays in the log, no matter how elegant the experimental design is.
