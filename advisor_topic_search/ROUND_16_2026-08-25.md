# Candidate 1 — Agreement Is Not Corroboration

## Working title

**Agreement Is Not Corroboration: Do Language Models Account for Source Dependence?**

## External construct

The epistemic value of agreement depends on whether reports are **independent observations** or merely repetitions / descendants of one underlying source.

This distinction exists independently of LLMs and is mature in cognitive science, testimony aggregation, Bayesian evidence integration, OSINT, and provenance analysis.

## Old scientific anchor

Yousif, Aboody, and Keil (Psychological Science, 2019) distinguish **true consensus** from **false consensus**. Human participants often treated multiple independent primary sources and repeated transmission of one primary source as similarly convincing.

- https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797619856844

Connor Desai, Xie, and Hayes (Cognition, 2022) further showed that people can distinguish true from false consensus when source independence is made sufficiently transparent.

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105023

A 2025 Cognition paper also models belief polarization arising from disagreements about source independence:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106126

## Why this is not an LLM-made problem

Even if every tested model is perfect, the scientific question remains meaningful: do artificial reasoners use **evidential provenance structure**, rather than merely the number and wording of agreeing reports?

Cross-model differences would be boundary conditions, not topic failure.

## Operationalization

Freeze:

- claim content;
- number of reports;
- report wording / stance;
- source reliability;
- total token count;
- number of explicit endorsements.

Manipulate only the provenance graph:

```text
4 reports / 4 independent roots
4 reports / 3 independent roots
4 reports / 2 independent roots
4 reports / 1 independent root
```

A stricter paired design can use identical report texts and only alter sentences describing how each report was produced or cited.

### DV

- posterior probability / confidence;
- accept / reject decision under a fixed loss threshold;
- marginal belief update per added report;
- calibration against a known generative model.

## Competing explanations

1. **Report-count heuristic:** four agreeing texts are treated as four pieces of evidence regardless of provenance.
2. **Repetition / fluency heuristic:** repeated agreement itself boosts confidence.
3. **Reliability-only heuristic:** the model notices source quality but not dependence.
4. **Provenance-sensitive integration:** dependent reports are discounted according to their shared evidential root.

## Frozen minimal G0

Programmatically generate binary claims with known likelihoods. For each claim, present four reports whose individual reliability is fixed. Compare belief update under 4-root and 1-root provenance while keeping the surface evidence count fixed.

Primary prerequisite signature:

```text
confidence(4 independent roots) > confidence(1 shared root)
```

and preferably a monotone relationship with effective root count.

No paid API, human annotation, or upstream benchmark reproduction is needed.

## Null-result interpretation

A null is scientifically interpretable: the tested models exhibit an **illusion of consensus**, treating repeated descendants of one source as if they were independent corroboration.

## Full-paper runway

- explicit vs implicit provenance;
- citation chains and copied/paraphrased reports;
- source reliability × source independence interaction;
- multilingual reporting;
- RAG / Deep Research synthesis;
- internal representation of provenance only after behavioral result is stable;
- dependency-aware evidence aggregation as an intervention.

## Collision audit

A fresh 2026 paper explicitly argues that source independence is measurable from provenance, so the paper cannot claim that “source independence matters for AI” is novel:

- Andre Byrd, *Source Independence Is a Measurable Property of Evidence* (2026): https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6903718

There is also very recent work on correlated false consensus among LLM judge panels, e.g. JuryProbe (2026), but this concerns **correlated model errors**, not whether a language model discounts externally dependent evidence reports:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20607

Targeted searches did not identify a mainstream NLP/ML paper that directly performs the matched **independent-root vs shared-root belief-update** experiment above.

## Status

`STRONG_KEEP / EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT_PASS / DIRECT_G0 / COLLISION_MEDIUM`

# Candidate 2 — When Is No Evidence Evidence?

## Working title

**When Is No Evidence Evidence? Do Language Models Reason from Informative Absence?**

## External construct

An absence is informative only relative to the probability that the missing observation **would have been detected if the hypothesis were true**.

This is a classic problem in Bayesian reasoning, cognitive science, forensics, diagnosis, search, archaeology, and scientific inference.

## Old scientific anchor

Hsu, Horng, Griffiths, and Chater (Cognitive Science, 2017) showed that human judgments about absent events track how surprising / diagnostic the absence is under a Bayesian account:

- https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12356

Forensic-science work similarly formalizes when failure to find a trace constitutes evidence against a hypothesis:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2019.05.044

## Why this is not an LLM-made problem

“Nothing was found” is not intrinsically evidence for nonexistence. The evidential force depends on detectability / coverage / search sensitivity. This problem exists regardless of model architecture.

## Operationalization

Fix:

- hypothesis prior;
- observation = no evidence found;
- surface wording and decision format.

Manipulate:

```text
P(detect evidence | H true)
= 0.10, 0.30, 0.50, 0.70, 0.90, 0.99
```

For each condition, an exact Bayesian posterior can be computed.

Then translate the same structure into natural scenarios:

- database / literature search;
- clinical screening;
- audit / fraud detection;
- archaeology;
- software testing;
- forensic trace search.

### DV

- posterior probability of H;
- confidence decrease after a null search;
- accept / reject / continue-search decision;
- ordinal monotonicity with detectability.

## Competing explanations

1. **Slogan heuristic:** “absence of evidence is never evidence of absence.”
2. **Search-effort heuristic:** any larger search description causes lower confidence, regardless of actual sensitivity.
3. **Domain heuristic:** medical / legal wording drives the answer.
4. **Diagnosticity-sensitive inference:** the update depends on how likely evidence would have been observed under H.

## Frozen minimal G0

Generate synthetic scenarios with known priors and detection probabilities. Observe no signal in every case.

Prerequisite signature:

```text
higher expected detectability
→ stronger negative belief update
```

The first G0 should test ordinal structure before exact numerical calibration.

## Null-result interpretation

A null is meaningful: models do not distinguish **uninformative missing evidence** from **surprising absence**, despite the underlying inference being fully specified.

## Full-paper runway

- implicit vs explicit detectability;
- stopping/search budget;
- negative search results in tool-using agents;
- multiple independent failed searches;
- source reliability and false-negative rates;
- whether models internally represent expected observation before final judgment;
- search policies that stop only after absence becomes sufficiently diagnostic.

## Collision audit

Targeted searches for `absence of evidence`, `informative absence`, and `evidence of absence` with LLM reasoning did not surface a direct NLP/ML behavioral study of this question. A 2026 paper with “Evidence of Absence” in the title concerns cross-modal risk perception under visual occlusion, not language-model epistemic reasoning:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14952

## Status

`STRONG_KEEP / EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT_PASS / DIRECT_G0 / COLLISION_LOW`

# Candidate 3 — Same Evidence, Different Sampling Process

## Working title

**Same Evidence, Different Sampling Process: Do Language Models Condition Generalization on How Examples Were Selected?**

## External construct

The informativeness of a sample depends not only on its contents but also on the **data-generating / selection process**.

A set of examples deliberately selected because they satisfy a concept (strong / property sampling) licenses different generalization from the same examples encountered randomly (weak / category sampling).

## Old scientific anchor

Navarro, Dry, and Lee (Cognitive Science, 2012) study strong vs weak sampling in inductive generalization:

- https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2011.01212.x

Hayes et al. (Cognition, 2024) show that people can update the assumed sampling frame even after the evidence has already been observed, including when an earlier sampling description is retracted and replaced:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2024.105717

## Why this is not an LLM-made problem

The same observations can rationally support different hypotheses depending on why those observations appeared in the sample. This is a general property of induction.

## Operationalization

Use novel synthetic categories / intervals so no world knowledge is needed.

Keep the observed examples identical, e.g.:

```text
observed positives: x = {31, 33, 35}
```

Manipulate only the sampling frame:

```text
STRONG: a teacher deliberately selected positive examples from the true concept.
WEAK: examples were selected randomly from the population and happened to be positive.
```

Then query the probability that new points / category members satisfy the property.

Second-stage diagnostic:

```text
sampling frame before evidence
vs
sampling frame revealed after evidence
vs
initial frame retracted and replaced
```

## Competing explanations

1. **Content-only induction:** same observations → same generalization.
2. **Instruction-parroting:** model changes answer only when wording explicitly says “generalize narrowly/broadly.”
3. **Pragmatic teacher-intent reasoning:** effects arise from cooperative-speaker assumptions.
4. **Sampling-aware induction:** model conditions likelihood / generalization on the data-generating process.

## Frozen minimal G0

Programmatically generate one-dimensional interval concepts where strong- and weak-sampling posteriors differ analytically. Use the same observed exemplars across paired prompts.

Prerequisite signature:

```text
same data + different sampling frame
→ systematically different generalization
```

with the direction matching the generative model.

## Null-result interpretation

A null says models treat evidence as a bag of observed examples and fail to condition induction on **why those examples were observed**.

## Full-paper runway

- property induction with semantic categories;
- retrospective sampling-frame revision;
- helpful vs adversarial selection;
- sampling-frame ambiguity;
- active learning / teaching contexts;
- scientific evidence and dataset curation;
- representation/mechanism only after behavioral sampling sensitivity is established.

## Collision audit

LLM property-induction papers already exist, so novelty cannot be “LLMs can perform category/property induction.” The proposed contribution must remain specifically about **sampling-process sensitivity under content-matched evidence**, especially retrospective revision.

The human literature also shows that the familiar diversity effect itself depends on sampling assumptions:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6558053/

Targeted searches did not identify a direct modern LLM study centered on the strong-vs-weak sampling manipulation above, but the broad induction area is not empty.

## Status

`KEEP / EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT_PASS / DIRECT_G0 / COLLISION_MEDIUM`

# Candidate 4 — The Same Fit Is Not the Same Evidence

## Working title

**The Same Fit Is Not the Same Evidence: Do Language Models Discount Data Reused for Hypothesis Generation?**

Alternative framing:

**Do LLM Scientific Reasoners Understand Selective Evidence?**

## External construct

Evidence used to **select / construct a hypothesis** cannot always be treated as an independent confirmatory test of that same hypothesis. The statistical version is selective inference / double dipping; the scientific-method version includes HARKing and post-hoc accommodation.

The important object is not the moral slogan “post hoc is bad,” but the dependence between:

```text
hypothesis-selection process
and
subsequent evidence evaluation
```

## Old scientific anchor

Kerr introduced HARKing in 1998:

- https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0203_4

Kriegeskorte et al. discuss circular analysis / double dipping and show how selection on noisy measurements can produce invalid inference:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2841687/

Modern selective inference explicitly treats data-dependent hypothesis/model selection as part of the inference problem. A concise overview from the University of Washington states the core issue: if the same data select and test the null hypothesis, ordinary tests fail to control selective Type-I error.

- https://stat.uw.edu/research/exams/testing-data-driven-hypotheses

## Why this is not an LLM-made problem

This is a long-standing question about scientific confirmation and statistical selection. LLMs are simply new scientific reasoners / agents to test.

## Operationalization

Construct paired scientific stories with the **same final reported fit / effect size**, but different hypothesis histories.

Example family:

```text
PRE-SPECIFIED:
one hypothesis H was fixed before seeing data D.
H obtains statistic s on D.

SELECTED:
M candidate hypotheses were inspected on D.
the best-fitting H* is selected.
H* obtains the same statistic s on D.
```

Manipulate M programmatically:

```text
1, 2, 5, 10, 50, 100 candidate hypotheses
```

Under a known null generator, the evidential value of the selected best fit can be calibrated exactly by simulation / selective probability.

A second paired test can compare:

```text
H generated on discovery data D1 and confirmed on held-out D2
vs
H generated and “confirmed” on the same D1
```

with matched headline statistics.

### DV

- judged evidential strength;
- posterior confidence in H;
- replication recommendation;
- whether another independent test is requested;
- calibration against selective-inference ground truth.

## Competing explanations

1. **Statistic-only heuristic:** same p-value / fit → same evidence.
2. **Post-hoc stigma heuristic:** any post-hoc story is discounted by a fixed amount regardless of search space.
3. **Preregistration keyword heuristic:** responds to labels such as “preregistered” without tracking selection structure.
4. **Selection-aware inference:** evidential weight changes systematically with the number / flexibility of hypotheses searched and independence of confirmatory data.

## Frozen minimal G0

Use a transparent synthetic null process. Sample M independent candidate hypotheses / statistics, select the maximum, and produce paired natural-language descriptions with identical final observed score s.

Prerequisite signatures:

```text
larger hypothesis search space
→ less confirmatory weight for the selected best fit
```

and

```text
held-out confirmation > same-data “confirmation”
```

No human labels are needed because the generative process gives the normative calibration target.

## Null-result interpretation

A null is strong: models evaluate scientific evidence from the reported result while ignoring the **selection process that produced the reported hypothesis**.

## Full-paper runway

- HARKing vs transparent exploration;
- multiple comparisons / garden of forking paths;
- model/hyperparameter selection;
- data snooping in scientific agents;
- preregistration and held-out confirmation;
- selective-inference-aware research agents;
- test whether internal state distinguishes “discovered on data” from “confirmed by data.”

## Collision audit

Targeted searches for `HARKing`, `double dipping`, `post hoc hypothesis`, and `selective inference` with LLM reasoning did not identify a direct systematic LLM evaluation of this construct.

There is abundant work on the statistical problem itself, including fresh 2026 work on post-hoc hypothesis testing, so the novelty must be about **whether LLM scientific reasoners represent and use selection history**, not a new statistical method:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2026.109634

## Status

`KEEP / EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT_PASS / DIRECT_G0 / COLLISION_LOW_MEDIUM`

# Candidate 5 — Cue Competition / Kamin Blocking

## Working title

**Does In-Context Association Exhibit Cue Competition? Kamin Blocking as a Diagnostic of Prediction-Error Learning**

## External construct

Cue competition is a classic associative-learning phenomenon. In Kamin blocking, prior learning that cue A predicts outcome X suppresses learning about a newly added cue B when the compound A+B continues to predict X.

The mother question is:

> Is context-level association governed by mere co-occurrence, or does it exhibit prediction-error / informativeness-sensitive learning signatures?

## Why this survives the new README gate

The research object is a mature learning construct, not an LLM artifact. Blocking, unblocking, and overshadowing can be specified before choosing any model.

## Operationalization

```text
BLOCKING
Phase 1: A → X
Phase 2: A+B → X
Test:    B → ?

CONTROL
matched B–X exposure
but without a previously established competing predictor A
```

The experiment must include more than one signature:

- blocking;
- unblocking when the outcome changes / prediction error returns;
- overshadowing or matched compound controls;
- exposure-count matching.

Otherwise generic recency/context interference can mimic “blocking.”

## Competing explanations

1. mere co-occurrence count;
2. attention / recency competition;
3. context-window interference;
4. prediction-error-sensitive cue competition.

## Frozen minimal G0

Synthetic symbols, balanced tokens, randomized mappings, multiple prompt serializations, and at least two model families before promotion.

Mechanism work is explicitly forbidden until the classical behavioral signature family is established.

## Null-result interpretation

A null is interpretable if the design is clean: current LLM in-context association does not show the hallmark blocking/unblocking structure expected from this class of error-driven learning accounts.

## Full-paper runway

- model family / scale / post-training as boundary conditions;
- blocking vs overshadowing vs unblocking;
- language learning analogues;
- comparison with explicit associative-learning models;
- internal prediction-error signatures only after behavior is established.

## Collision audit — serious

EMNLP 2025 already introduced a Rescorla–Wagner-inspired analysis and steering method for competing mixed contexts in LLMs:

- Wang et al., *Rescorla-Wagner Steering of LLMs for Undesired Behaviors over Disproportionate Inappropriate Context*: https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1003/

That paper does **not** appear to run the classical Kamin blocking → unblocking diagnostic, but it occupies the broader “RW / associative learning explains LLM context weighting” narrative.

Therefore we must not sell this as “first application of associative learning / RW to LLMs.” The only defensible version is the narrower scientific question about **hallmark learning-rule signatures and their boundary conditions**.

## Status

`CONDITIONAL_KEEP / EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT_PASS / COLLISION_MEDIUM_HIGH`

- https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.303/

This lane is not dead forever, but a simple “do LLMs understand common knowledge?” formulation is already too occupied.

Also natural, but 2026 is already crowded with explicit LLM-agent source-monitoring / provenance work, including:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25869
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26778

Do not re-enter through a generic “does the model remember where a fact came from?” question.

## Temporal fact mutability

Already directly occupied by recent temporal knowledge-conflict work; do not narrow it merely to recover novelty.

## Generic belief revision / defeasible reasoning

Too much direct benchmark and reasoning literature. Only return if a genuinely external construct yields a new mother question rather than a narrower benchmark slice.

## Generic source credibility / epistemic vigilance

Source reliability is already an active LLM research axis. Candidate 1 survives because **dependence / shared provenance** is a distinct variable and is held apart from reliability.
