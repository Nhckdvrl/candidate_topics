# Advisor Topic Search — Round 01 (2026-08-23)

## Objective

Reset the search specifically around topics the advisor could plausibly accept, rather than importing frontier-AI interests from the personal track.

The search source is the lab's actual 2025–2026 work: character-level acquisition, semantic frames, embeddings, quiz science, citation/scientific-document analysis, specialized generation, multilingual factors, social-survey replication, and language/model internals.

This round does **not** register any numbered topic. It only defines promising object families and rejects obvious mismatches.

---

## A1. Question/quiz structure as a scientific object

**Status:** `HIGH_PRIORITY_SEARCH_LANE`

Why advisor-fit:

- the lab has an established quiz / early-answering line;
- question, clue, answer, human accuracy and answer timing are naturally defined objects;
- many quantities can be measured without an LLM judge;
- human-vs-model differences create direct empirical phenomena.

What to mine next:

- clue-order effects that change LLM and human answerability differently;
- questions where more information paradoxically hurts one side;
- late clues that cause answer reversal rather than simple confidence growth;
- systematic differences between human difficulty and model difficulty that can be attributed to question structure rather than broad topic/domain;
- distractor or ambiguity structure that produces qualitatively different error modes.

Reject:

- another flat 'LLM vs human quiz accuracy' paper;
- dataset-only work;
- a topic requiring hand annotation of vague 'reasoning types'.

## A2. Acquisition trajectories of a concrete property in open checkpoints

**Status:** `HIGH_PRIORITY_SEARCH_LANE`

Why advisor-fit:

- directly adjacent to the lab's EACL 2026 character-level acquisition work and checkpoint-rich projects;
- clean developmental x-axis;
- can yield strong plots if the property has a surprising acquisition order or non-monotonicity.

Hard constraint:

Do not reuse current labmate objects such as character-level information, typoglycemia, or onomatopoeia.

Search for properties with:

- automatic test generation;
- precise ground truth;
- clear pre-existing linguistic/cognitive significance;
- potential dissociation across surface form / meaning / structure;
- a reason acquisition order would matter scientifically.

Bad form:

> maybe property X emerges before property Y.

Good form:

> prior literature already reports a sharp asymmetry/failure for property X; open checkpoints let us locate when that known phenomenon appears.

## A3. Human-vs-LLM difficulty disagreement with a structural explanation

**Status:** `DEEP_AUDIT`

A recent lab line already asks whether humans and LLMs find the same Japanese quiz questions difficult. The adjacent space is only interesting if we move from correlation to a **specific question property that explains large disagreement**.

Promising observable families:

- clue redundancy;
- temporal/clue-position dependence;
- lexical rarity vs relational composition;
- answer popularity/frequency;
- distractor density;
- entity alias ambiguity;
- local clue informativeness.

The target should be a direct statement like:

> a concrete question property systematically creates 'easy-for-human / hard-for-LLM' cases.

Do not invent a latent cognitive explanation until the property-level phenomenon exists.

## A4. Embedding compression failures that preserve benchmark score but destroy a concrete capability

**Status:** `DEEP_AUDIT`

Lab fit is strong because recent work studies redundancy, isotropy, intrinsic dimensionality, dimensionality reduction and quantization.

Potential phenomenon shape:

> global retrieval/similarity metrics remain almost unchanged under aggressive compression, while one specific structural relation collapses abruptly.

The object must be concrete and externally meaningful, e.g.:

- neighborhood stability for rare entities;
- compositional relations;
- negation/antonym separation;
- cross-lingual alignment;
- fine-grained ranking consistency.

Risk:

This can easily become another profile/correlation story like archived Topic12. Require a direct task-level failure, not 'geometry changes'.

---

## A5. Scientific-document structure with programmatic/low-ontology labels

**Status:** `KEEP_LANE, STRONG WARNING`

Advisor fit is strong: the lab has citation-importance and related-work citation organization projects, plus domain scientific information extraction.

But Topics 16 and 17 show exactly what not to do: do not define a research object that requires multi-step semantic provenance or bespoke method ontologies before it can be measured.

Prefer document questions with structural labels such as:

- citation placement/order;
- repeated citation chains;
- section-level citation movement;
- reference reuse patterns;
- explicit contradiction/retraction/correction markers;
- whether a cited result is actually present in table/abstract/conclusion under narrowly defined cases.

Need a phenomenon stronger than classification accuracy.

---

## A6. Semantic-frame resource inconsistencies / coverage phenomena

**Status:** `WATCH`

Strong lab neighborhood, but easy to collide with FrameEOL / FrameBench / definition generation.

Only search for an empirical phenomenon not already equal to 'better frame induction'. Examples of shapes:

- systematic frame splitting/merging disagreement across resources;
- lexical units whose frame assignment is unstable under paraphrase;
- definition information that predicts annotation disagreement;
- coverage holes concentrated in a specific natural class.

The object should be resource-level and auditable, not another generic LLM evaluator.

---

## A7. Specialized structured generation where models satisfy meaning but violate domain form

**Status:** `WATCH`

This matches patent-claim translation and detailed Kanji-description style projects: the output has explicit structural conventions beyond ordinary semantics.

Potential source domains:

- patent claims;
- legal/technical definitions;
- dictionary-style descriptions;
- procedural scientific text;
- constrained summaries with required relation structure.

Interesting phenomenon shape:

> models preserve propositional content while systematically violating one domain-defining structural constraint, and the violation has a measurable downstream consequence.

Do not do generic 'LLM struggles with domain X'.

---

## A8. LLM-as-instrument for scaling an old empirical question

**Status:** `HIGH_POTENTIAL, MEASUREMENT_RISK`

This is advisor-compatible when the old question is concrete and human annotation historically limited scale.

Good domains may include:

- metaphor/figurative-language hypothesis verification (already a lab precedent);
- large-scale question-quality/difficulty analysis;
- semantic resource audits;
- scientific-document organization;
- social-science response pattern replication.

But Topic16 gives the key kill rule:

> if the LLM instrument cannot reproduce known cases with high reliability, stop before large-scale discovery.
